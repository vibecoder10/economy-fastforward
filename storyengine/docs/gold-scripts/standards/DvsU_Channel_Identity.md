# DvsU — Channel Identity & Editorial Constitution
## The single-page foundation of every editorial decision

---

## WHAT DvsU IS

**DvsU (Designed vs Used)** is an engineering documentary channel.

The channel is not about war.
The channel is not about weapons.
The channel is not about military history.

**The channel is about machines.**

More specifically: about the engineering decisions that made those machines what they were — and what happened when those decisions met reality.

---

## THE ONE-LINE IDENTITY

> **Controversial engineering without drama.**

Every word matters:

**Controversial** — the machines DvsU covers have stories worth arguing about. Design failures, procurement disasters, doctrinal mistakes, engineering trade-offs that went wrong. Not "here is a famous aircraft." But "here is why this aircraft was wrong — and what that cost."

**Engineering** — the subject is always the machine's design, not its combat record. A battle is only interesting if it reveals something about the engineering. A pilot's death is only relevant if it exposes a design flaw.

**Without drama** — no hype. No "THIS IS INSANE." No YouTube exaggeration. Calm, precise, authoritative. The audience is 55+, serious, and they fact-check everything. Drama loses them. Authority keeps them.

---

## THE STRATEGIC FRAME

> **"The Most Controversial / Hated / Failed / Legendary Machines Ever Built"**

This frame governs which topics DvsU covers and how.

Every video must fit one of four angles:

| Angle | What it means | Example titles |
|-------|--------------|----------------|
| **Controversial** | Engineering decisions that divided opinion | Every US Aircraft That Changed Aviation / Never-Built |
| **Hated** | Machines despised by the people who operated them | Most Hated Fighter Jets / Most Hated Tanks by Their Crews |
| **Failed** | Designs that didn't work — and why | Every Failed US Bomber / Never-Built US Destroyers |
| **Legendary** | Machines that defined their era | Every US Strategic Bomber / Every US Aircraft Carrier |

A video that fits none of these four angles does not belong on DvsU.

---

## THE AUDIENCE

**Primary:** Men, 55+, English-speaking, serious military history enthusiasts.

They have read the books. They have watched the documentaries. Many served. Many worked in aviation, naval, or defence industries.

They do not need to be impressed. They need to be informed — and occasionally surprised by something they did not already know.

**What they want:**
- Accurate information presented with authority
- Engineering insight they haven't encountered before
- Respect for their existing knowledge
- Occasionally: a fact or story that contradicts what they thought they knew

**What drives them away:**
- Factual errors
- Hype and exaggeration
- Obvious facts presented as revelations
- Catalog-style lists without insight

---

## THE EDITORIAL LAWS

These are not guidelines. They are laws. Every document in the DvsU production system exists to enforce them.

**1. The machine is always the subject. Never the war.**
A ship is interesting because of what it was designed to do and what happened when it tried. Not because of the battle it fought in.

**2. Every unit must have a unique engineering story.**
If two units would tell the same engineering story — remove one. A catalog entry is not a DvsU unit.

**3. The paragraph is about the decision, not the machine.**
A 95–120 word paragraph describes why this machine exists and what it reveals — not what it weighs and how fast it goes.

**4. Facts serve the story. The story does not serve the facts.**
No orphan facts. Every technical detail answers one of three questions: why was it designed this way / what problem did it solve / what consequence did it create.

**5. Accuracy is non-negotiable.**
The audience fact-checks everything. One error damages the entire channel's credibility. Hedge uncertain claims. Remove unverifiable claims. Never manufacture controversy.

**6. The visual identity is fixed.**
White background. One machine. 2–4 words. One red accent. Red arrow. Never redesign.

**7. The voice is fixed.**
Nathaniel C, Multilingual v2, fixed settings. Consistent across every episode.

**8. Quality determines quantity. Never the reverse.**
18 strong units is better than 30 weak ones. A topic that cannot produce 15 strong units is not ready.

**9. The video ends on the final unit.**
No conclusion paragraph. No summary. The final sentence of the final unit closes the thesis.

**10. The audience already knows the machine exists.**
The paragraph never proves existence. It reveals significance.

---

## WHAT DvsU IS NOT

These are production guardrails. If a piece of content violates any of these — it does not belong on the channel.

❌ Not a war documentary — battles are context, never the subject
❌ Not a weapons review — firepower is irrelevant unless it reveals an engineering failure
❌ Not a history lecture — chronology is structure, never the content
❌ Not a ranked list — every unit earns its place through a unique story
❌ Not a Wikipedia article — no paragraph should read like an encyclopedia entry
❌ Not clickbait — no manufactured controversy, no exaggeration, no hype

---

## THE THESIS RULE

Every video must have one thesis — one argument about engineering that the entire video exists to prove.

**Not:** "Here are 28 US battleships."
**Yes:** "America kept building battleships for a war that no longer existed — and each one was obsolete before it was finished."

If the thesis cannot be stated in one sentence before the first paragraph is written — the video is not ready to write.
