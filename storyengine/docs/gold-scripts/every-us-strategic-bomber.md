## Scene 1 (79 words)

The Wittemann-Lewis XNBL-1, known as the Barling Bomber, was an experimental long-range heavy bomber built for the United States Army Air Service in the early 1920s. The designation XNBL-1 stood for Experimental Night Bomber, Long Range. Designer Walter Barling, an Englishman who had worked on similar large bomber concepts for the Royal Aircraft Factory during World War I, gave the aircraft its popular nickname. The triplane was powered by six 420hp Liberty L-12 engines and was retired in 1928.

## Scene 2 (102 words)

The Boeing XB-15 was a United States bomber aircraft designed in 1934 as a test for the United States Army Air Corps to see if it would be possible to build a heavy bomber with a 5,000 mi range. Boeing gave the project the internal name Model 294. The prototype first flew on 15 October 1937. It had a 149-foot wingspan and accommodations for two complete crews. Unusual features included an autopilot, deicing equipment, and two gasoline generators used as auxiliary power units to power the 110-volt electrical system. The USAAC later converted the XB-15 to a cargo carrier, designated the XC-105.

## Scene 3 (100 words)

The Boeing B-17 Flying Fortress was a four-engine heavy bomber produced for the Army Air Corps prior to and during World War II. Developed in response to a 1934 US Army Air Corps requirement for a multi-engine coastal defence bomber, the B-17 first flew on 28 July 1935 and entered service in 1938. The aircraft saw early action in the Pacific War, conducting air raids against Japanese shipping and airfields. Its most significant service was over Europe, where the 8th Air Force flew the B-17 on missions deep into Germany targeting aircraft factories, ball-bearing plants, oil refineries, and rail infrastructure.

## Scene 4 (99 words)

The Consolidated B-24 Liberator was a four-engine heavy bomber produced for the Army Air Corps prior to and during World War II. At its inception, the B-24 featured a modern design with a highly efficient shoulder-mounted, high aspect ratio Davis wing. The aircraft was developed in response to a request from the United States Army Air Corps for a bomber with longer range and higher operational ceiling than the B-17 Flying Fortress. The B-24 was used extensively in World War II, serving in every branch of the American armed forces, as well as several Allied air forces and navies.

## Scene 5 (96 words)

Douglas XB-19 was a four-engined, piston-driven heavy bomber produced by Douglas Aircraft Company for the United States Army Air Forces during the early 1940s. The project was intended to test flight characteristics and design techniques for giant bombers. Despite advances in technology that made the XB-19 obsolete before it was completed, the Army Air Corps believed the prototype would be useful for testing. It made its first flight on 27 June 1941. After completion of testing, the XB-19 was earmarked for conversion into a cargo aircraft, but modifications were not completed. The aircraft was subsequently scrapped.

## Scene 6 (64 words)

Boeing B-29 Superfortress was a US heavy bomber aircraft that served from 1944 to 1960. Wartime production was spread among Boeing plants in Wichita, Kansas and Renton, Washington and built under license by Martin and Bell. During the war in the Pacific Theater, the B-29 delivered the first nuclear weapons used in combat. The B-29 continued in frontline bomber service through the Korean War.

## Scene 7 (85 words)

Consolidated B-32 Dominator was an American heavy strategic bomber built for the United States Army Air Forces during World War II. It was developed in parallel with the Boeing B-29 Superfortress as a fallback design should the Superfortress prove unsuccessful. The B-32 was powered by four Wright R-3350-23 Duplex Cyclone 18-cylinder air-cooled radial piston engines with two turbochargers. The aircraft reached units in the Pacific only in mid-May 1945 and subsequently saw only limited combat operations against Japanese targets before the end of the war.

## Scene 8 (95 words)

Northrop YB-35 was an experimental heavy bomber developed by the Northrop Corporation for the United States Army Air Forces during and shortly after World War II. The aircraft featured a flying wing design, eliminating the conventional fuselage and tail. It was powered by four Pratt & Whitney R-4360 radial engines driving contra-rotating propellers. The first YB-35 flew on 25 June 1946. Technical problems including propeller vibration and gearbox malfunctions plagued the program. The AAF ordered Northrop to convert two YB-35 airframes to jet-powered YB-49s. The program was ultimately canceled, with the aircraft retired by 1948-1949.

## Scene 9 (83 words)

Northrop YB-49 was a jet-powered flying wing heavy bomber prototype developed by Northrop Corporation for the United States Air Force. It was a turbojet-powered development of the earlier piston-engined Northrop XB-35 and YB-35. The aircraft was powered by eight Allison J35 turbojet engines. A YB-49 prototype crashed on 5 June 1948, killing all five crew members, including Captain Glen Edwards, for whom Edwards Air Force Base was later named. The YB-49 never entered production, being passed over in favor of the Convair B-36.

## Scene 10 (84 words)

The Convair XB-46 was an experimental medium jet bomber developed in the mid-1940s as a United States response to German jet bomber developments. It featured a streamlined fuselage with four Chevrolet-built J35-C3 turbojet engines paired in nacelles under straight shoulder-mounted wings. Only one prototype was constructed, completed as a stripped-down aircraft lacking mission equipment. The XB-46 made its first flight on 2 April 1947. The project was cancelled with the success of the Boeing B-47, and the aircraft never entered production or active service.

## Scene 11 (90 words)

The North American B-45 Tornado was the first jet-propelled bomber to enter service with the United States Air Force. Designed and manufactured by North American Aviation, it first flew on 17 March 1947 and entered service on 22 April 1948. The aircraft featured a straight wing and four turbojet engines mounted in two underwing nacelles. It served in both strategic bomber and reconnaissance roles during the early Cold War, including reconnaissance missions during the Korean War. The B-45 was eventually superseded by the larger and more capable Boeing B-47 Stratojet.

## Scene 12 (83 words)

The Boeing B-47 Stratojet was a six-engine medium strategic bomber produced by Boeing. Its primary mission was as a nuclear bomber capable of striking targets within the Soviet Union. Development can be traced back to a 1943 United States Army Air Forces requirement for a reconnaissance bomber harnessing newly developed jet propulsion. With its engines carried in nacelles underneath the wing, the B-47 represented a major innovation in post–World War II combat jet design and contributed to the development of modern jet airliners.

## Scene 13 (90 words)

Martin XB-48 was an American medium jet bomber developed in the mid-1940s. Designed from Air Force specifications issued in 1944, it competed against the North American XB-45, Convair XB-46, and Boeing XB-47. The XB-48 was Martin's first jet bomber and featured a six-engine straight-wing configuration. It employed bicycle-type tandem landing gear, which had previously been tested on a modified B-26. The aircraft made its first flight on 22 June 1947. The program was cancelled in 1948, as the XB-48's top speed of 516 mph fell short of the XB-47's performance.

## Scene 14 (101 words)

The Convair B-36 Peacemaker was a strategic bomber built for the United States Air Force, serving from 1949 to 1959. Originally designed to attack Nazi-controlled Germany from US East Coast bases if Great Britain became occupied, the aircraft arrived too late for World War II. The B-36 was powered by six Pratt & Whitney R-4360 Wasp Major radial piston engines, and beginning with the B-36D, the Peacemaker used six radial piston engines and four jet engines. It was capable of intercontinental flight without refueling and could fly missions to targets 3,400 mi away and stay aloft as long as 40 hours.

## Scene 15 (82 words)

The North American AJ Savage was a U.S. Navy carrier-based medium bomber built by North American Aviation. It first flew on 3 July 1948 and was introduced in 1950, serving until retirement in 1960. The aircraft featured twin radial piston engines and a single jet engine. With a wingspan of over 70 feet, the Savage was an imposing sight for carrier landing signal officers and flight deck crews. The Navy adapted the AJ Savage design to accommodate the Mark 4 nuclear bomb.

## Scene 16 (94 words)

Boeing B-50 Superfortress is an American strategic bomber that is a post–World War II revision of the Boeing B-29 Superfortress. It was fitted with more powerful Pratt & Whitney R-4360 radial engines, a stronger internal structure, and a taller tail fin. The aircraft made its first flight on June 25, 1947, and entered operational service in 1948 with the newly formed Strategic Air Command. After its primary bomber service ended, many B-50 airframes were converted to aerial tankers (KB-50) and weather reconnaissance aircraft (WB-50). The B-50 was in USAF service for nearly 20 years.

## Scene 17 (104 words)

Convair YB-60 was a prototype heavy bomber built by Convair for the United States Air Force in the early 1950s. Developed from the Convair B-36, the aircraft featured swept wings and eight underslung jet engines. The YB-60 had 72% parts commonality with its piston-engined predecessor. Its first flight occurred on April 18, 1952, with Beryl Erickson as pilot. The YB-60 was notably slower than the competing YB-52, cruising at about 100 mph less, and encountered significant handling issues. The Air Force concluded that the YB-60's drawbacks outweighed the benefits of its increased payload, and the flight test program was terminated on January 20, 1953.

## Scene 18 (86 words)

Douglas A-3 Skywarrior was a jet-powered strategic bomber developed and produced by the Douglas Aircraft Company for the United States Navy. The prototype designated XA3D-1 conducted its first flight on October 28, 1952. The aircraft was introduced in 1956 and manufactured through 1961. With the advent of the Polaris missile, the Navy's carrier-based heavy attack mission faded and the versatile A3D, redesignated A-3 in 1962, assumed other roles and missions, which included aerial refueling, photoreconnaissance, and electronic countermeasures. The Skywarrior was retired on 27 September 1991.

## Scene 19 (102 words)

The Boeing B-52 Stratofortress is an American nuclear-capable subsonic jet-powered strategic bomber designed and built by Boeing. Initially designed in the late 1940s, the aircraft was engineered to provide the United States with a significant advantage in strategic nuclear capabilities during the Cold War. The B-52's design incorporated innovations such as aerial refueling and turbojet engines, with the first prototype taking flight on April 15, 1952. Built to carry nuclear weapons for Cold War deterrence missions, the B-52 Stratofortress replaced the Convair B-36 Peacemaker. The B-52 has been the backbone of U.S. Air Force strategic deterrence since it became operational in 1955.

## Scene 20 (100 words)

The Convair B-58 Hustler was a supersonic strategic bomber designed and produced by Convair for the United States Air Force Strategic Air Command during the 1950s. It was designed to replace the Boeing B-47 Stratojet. The B-58 entered service in March 1960 and was operated for a decade by two SAC bomb wings: the 43d Bombardment Wing and the 305th Bombardment Wing. Originally intended to fly at high altitudes and supersonic speeds to avoid Soviet interceptors, the introduction of Soviet high-altitude surface-to-air missiles forced the B-58 to adopt a low-level penetration role that severely limited its range and strategic value.

## Scene 21 (86 words)

The Martin P6M SeaMaster was a strategic bomber flying boat built by the Glenn L. Martin Company for the United States Navy. It was developed in the early Cold War to fill the need for a strategic nuclear weapon delivery system for the Navy. The Navy promoted the P6M primarily as a high speed minelayer. The design incorporated a rotating bomb bay pneumatically sealed against seawater. The first flight of the XP6M-1 came on 14 July 1955. The program was cancelled before it reached operational service.

## Scene 22 (104 words)

The North American A-5 Vigilante was a carrier-based supersonic bomber designed and built by North American Aviation for the United States Navy. Development began in 1954 as a private venture to produce a supersonic long-distance bomber as a successor to the North American XA2J Super Savage. The aircraft was designed to carry nuclear payloads including the Mk 27, B28, and B43 series of free-fall nuclear bombs. However, its original mission as a nuclear strike bomber was short-lived due to strategic changes, including the shift to intercontinental ballistic missiles as the primary delivery method for nuclear weapons. The A-5 never delivered a bomb in anger.

## Scene 23 (99 words)

North American XB-70 Valkyrie was an experimental supersonic strategic bomber prototype built by North American Aviation. The aircraft grew out of the 1950s Weapon System 110A requirement for a Mach 3 intercontinental bomber to replace the B-52. Originally conceived as a high-altitude nuclear strike bomber capable of flight at three times the speed of sound, the XB-70A was designed so that any potential enemy would have been unable to defend against it. The six-engine stainless-steel delta was designed to ride its own shock wave across a continent. Only two were built, and the program was cancelled before operational service.

## Scene 24 (104 words)

The General Dynamics FB-111A Aardvark was the strategic/theater bomber version of the F-111 multi-purpose aircraft. It was specially designed as a strategic bomber, carrying nuclear weapons at high speed and over long ranges. The FB-111A differed from tactical strike variants by having different engines, a 3 1/2-ft extension on each wingtip, strengthened landing gear and fuselage, greater fuel capacity, and improved avionics. Seventy-six of this model were built to bridge the gap between the retirement of the B-58 Hustler and the arrival of the B-1 Lancer. The FB-111A served with the Strategic Air Command at bases including Carswell, Plattsburgh, and Pease Air Force Bases.

## Scene 25 (95 words)

Rockwell B-1A Lancer was a swing-wing strategic bomber of blended wing-body design intended for high-speed, low-altitude penetration missions. The program originated from a 1965 USAF study to examine replacement of the B-70 Valkyrie and the B-52. Compared to the B-52, the B-1 used shorter runways, could carry twice the payload, and had a smaller radar profile. The B-1A featured variable aspect engine intakes which moved to give the best performance at a variety of speeds. The fourth and last B-1A was primarily used by the USAF as the avionics test bed for the B-1B program.

## Scene 26 (81 words)

Rockwell B-1B Lancer features a blended wing and body configuration, variable-geometry wings, and turbofan afterburning engines that combine to provide long range, maneuverability, and high speed. The aircraft was fitted with an on-board self-protection and electronic jamming suite, including an ALQ-161 radar warning receiver and associated countermeasures. The multi-mission B-1 is the backbone of America's long-range bomber force. Although B-1s were fully operational when Operations Desert Shield/Storm took place in 1990-1991, they were not used in any capacity during that conflict.

## Scene 27 (86 words)

The Northrop Grumman B-2 Spirit is a strategic, long-range heavy bomber with low-observable technology and all-altitude capability to penetrate the most sophisticated air defenses in nuclear and conventional missions. It is powered by four General Electric F118-GE-100 turbofans, each producing 17,300 lbs of thrust. The B-2 combines long-range, stealth and a heavy payload on a single platform. For three decades, the B-2 Spirit has been the backbone of stealth technology for the U.S. Air Force. The aircraft saw its first operational use during Operation Allied Force.

## Scene 28 (102 words)

The Northrop Grumman B-21 Raider is the next evolution of the Air Force strategic bomber fleet. Benefitting from more than three decades of strike and stealth technology innovation, the aircraft incorporates next-generation stealth technologies informed by decades of operational experience. The B-21 is designed to hold any target at risk, anywhere in the world, and will penetrate the toughest defenses for precision strikes. Its open architecture will deliver seamless upgrades, enabling the Raider fleet to evolve its mission and weapons capabilities to outpace any threat. Modernized, low-observable processes will also make the B-21 easier and less costly to maintain than prior systems.
