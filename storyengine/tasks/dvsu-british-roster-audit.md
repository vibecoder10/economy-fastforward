# Historical roster audit — Every British Aircraft Carrier Class Ever Built (2026)

Audit date: 2026-09-12
Input audited: `/tmp/dvsu-british-live.jsonl`
Scope: research only; no live-video, automation, or paid-job changes.

## Result

The live 15-entry roster is **not complete** under its own treatment of conversions and singleton designs. It omits five unambiguous British carrier types and one real boundary case:

1. **HMS Vindictive (1918) — singleton conversion — add.** She was completed and commissioned as an aircraft carrier, with a forward flying-off deck and an aft landing deck. Calling her a deckless seaplane carrier is false and is inconsistent with including Furious in her early split-deck form.
2. **HMS Activity — singleton escort-carrier conversion — add.** British-built merchant hull, taken over before completion and converted in Britain into a commissioned Royal Navy escort carrier.
3. **Nairana class — HMS Nairana and HMS Vindex — add.** Two British-built escort carriers. Vindex was not an exact duplicate, but the Royal Navy Research Archive treats both as the Nairana class.
4. **HMS Campania (1943) — singleton escort-carrier design — add.** British-built and converted before completion; similar to Nairana but catalogued separately.
5. **HMS Pretoria Castle — singleton escort-carrier conversion — add.** British-built liner converted in a British dockyard into a commissioned escort carrier.
6. **HMS Audacity — singleton escort-carrier conversion — recommended add, with the identity rule stated.** Her merchant hull was German-built and captured, but the Admiralty created the aircraft-carrier design and completed the carrier conversion at Blyth. She was the Royal Navy's first operational escort carrier. Include her if “British carrier” follows the carrier design/conversion, as the current roster already does for conversions. Exclude her only under a strict original-hull-country rule.

This produces a **working 21-entry roster** under the British carrier-design/conversion boundary, or 20 if Audacity is excluded because her original merchant hull was German-built. These are scope-dependent working counts, **not a completeness certification while the title's meaning of “British” remains unlocked**. The five other additions are required under the British-built/design interpretation.

## Recommended boundary

Use: **Every distinct aircraft-carrier design or conversion completed under a British programme in a British yard, with at least one ship commissioned as an aircraft carrier; include British classes completed for export.** Exclude seaplane-only carriers, Merchant Aircraft Carriers (MAC ships), helicopter-only assault ships, zero-hull paper/cancelled classes, and US-built/US-converted Lend-Lease escort-carrier classes.

This rule keeps **Majestic** (British class and construction, although none commissioned into the Royal Navy), excludes the US-built Archer/Avenger/Attacker/Ruler families, and includes the five British-built escort-carrier types above. It also gives a principled answer on Audacity: its carrier conversion was British even though its pre-conversion merchant hull was German.

### Royal-Navy-operated alternative

If “British” instead means **every aircraft-carrier class operated by or commissioned into the Royal Navy**, this is a different roster and the 20/21 working count must not be used. That scope would add the foreign-built Lend-Lease escort-carrier families (at minimum the Archer, Avenger, Attacker and later Bogue/Ruler-family groupings) and Audacity, while **Majestic would not qualify merely from British design/build because none served in the Royal Navy**. British wartime sources and later catalogues vary in whether Ameer, Ruler and Smiter are presented as classes, batches or subclasses, so that alternative needs its own locked taxonomy audit before any “complete” claim or number is published.

Do not mix the two identities. “British-built/design” can include Majestic and exclude US-built Lend-Lease classes; “Royal-Navy-operated” does the reverse. The current live contract mixes both by saying Royal Navy commissioning while retaining Majestic.

Do not describe escort carriers as MAC ships. Commissioned escort carriers were warships with naval crews; MAC ships remained cargo-carrying merchant vessels under the Red Ensign with Fleet Air Arm detachments. The live exclusion currently collapses these separate categories.

## Corrections inside retained entries

- **Furious/Courageous:** Keep Furious separate. The National Maritime Museum archive describes Courageous and Glorious as the Courageous-class aircraft carriers and lists Furious separately in the same ships cover. Replace “first of Courageous-class conversions” with “separate, earlier conversion of a Courageous-class-origin battlecruiser hull.”
- **Illustrious:** Keep **four completed carriers** in the operational roster: Illustrious, Formidable, Victorious and the modified Indomitable. Remove “4 completed out of 6 planned.” The other two ships often conflated with a six-ship total were the separately catalogued **Implacable class**, and both were completed; they were not cancelled Illustrious hulls. A taxonomy footnote may say the Admiralty archive groups the first three tightly and Indomitable was a modified fourth.
- **Implacable:** Two completed: Implacable and Indefatigable. National Maritime Museum Admiralty records explicitly catalogue them as the two Implacable-class ships.
- **Colossus:** The fact sheet's “8 ships” is misleading. State **10 class hulls completed: eight as light fleet carriers and two, Pioneer and Perseus, as aircraft-maintenance carriers**. Keeping Pioneer/Perseus within Colossus is defensible; no extra class entry is required.
- **Majestic:** State **six ships in the class; five completed/commissioned as carriers, while Leviathan was launched but never completed**. Do not say the class served in the Royal Navy. Official Australian naval history calls Melbourne one of six Majestic-class carriers and identifies British construction.
- **Audacious:** Keep **two completed** carriers, Eagle and Ark Royal. Four ships were covered by the 1942 large-fleet-carrier specification; Africa and the later cancelled Eagle were not completed. The class entry is valid because two real ships were completed. Do not let cancelled sisters invalidate the built class.
- **Centaur:** Keep **four completed** ships: Centaur, Albion, Bulwark and Hermes (laid down as Elephant). Four further orders were cancelled. The cancelled original Hermes is different from completed Elephant renamed Hermes. Hansard in 1952 explicitly referred to four “Hermes class” light fleet carriers under construction; National Maritime Museum plans identify the four completed hulls together.
- **Invincible:** Three completed is correct.
- **Queen Elizabeth:** Two completed and commissioned is correct.

## Decision for the parent repair

Treat the live `is_complete_title: true`, empty `gaps`, and 15-class claim as false. Before asserting a new complete count, lock whether the title means British-built/design or Royal-Navy-operated. Under the British-built/design interpretation, add Vindictive, Activity, Nairana, Campania and Pretoria Castle; recommended: also add Audacity because the carrier conversion was British. Under the Royal-Navy-operated interpretation, run the separate foreign-built escort-class taxonomy audit described above and remove Majestic unless the title explicitly includes British export designs. Preserve the completed **4 Illustrious / 2 Audacious / 4 Centaur** facts; repair the wording around cancelled orders rather than removing those classes.
