STORYBOARD SHEET — SCENE 3 — 9 PANELS  (v2)

This is a storyboard sheet: a grid of 9 panels in 3 columns and 3 rows on a plain light grey page. Each panel is a wide 16:9 cinematic frame — never square, never tall. Every panel uses the same art style: photorealistic cinematic film still, with matching lighting and colour grading throughout. A small plain strip below each panel shows only its panel number and shot type. The panel artwork contains no lettering, signs, speech bubbles, captions or written words of any kind — only the small panel-number label.

CHARACTER — stated once, drawn identically in every panel: Nyla, a young woman with shoulder-length black shaggy hair, wearing a charcoal grey zip-up bodysuit. She is ALONE in this scene: exactly one person in every panel, no other figures anywhere, and no sleeping bodies visible inside her own pod.

SET — every panel takes place at Nyla's glass bubble-pod. THE POD INTERIOR: a sealed transparent sphere. Upholstered bed frame-LEFT against the curved wall, rounded armchair CENTRE-RIGHT beside a low cylindrical coffee table, desk with storage FAR RIGHT against the curve, circular floor mat CENTRE, potted plant in the upper-LEFT wall niche, small glowing sphere lamp on the coffee table. A human-sized round hatch sits closed in the frame-LEFT curve, its sill flush with the catwalk outside. Cold blue-white LED light from the dome overhead.
THE DOME CEILING is the subject of this scene and must be visible in every interior panel that is not a macro insert. IT IS THE SAME TRANSPARENT SPHERE AS EVERY OTHER SCENE — never an opaque white shell: curved panes of clear glass through which the dark warren and the faint glow of neighbouring pods are visible above her, held in a framework of narrow pale STRUCTURAL RIBS that meet at a hub directly above the pod's centre. The ribs are the only solid surfaces overhead: narrow, matte pale white, carrying small flush fittings along them — vents, light strips, sensor points — all the same pale white, all unremarkable. The glass stays glass; the ribs are where anything mounted overhead lives.
THE WARREN OUTSIDE, visible only through the glass and in the final exterior panel: towering stacks of other cylindrical pods glowing faintly, wet reflective catwalk plating, blue-white glow receding into darkness.

THE LENS — READ THIS CAREFULLY, IT IS THE POINT OF THE SCENE: set flush into one of the dome's pale structural ribs, close to the hub directly above the centre of the pod, there is a single small dark glass bead — a hidden camera lens, about the size of a fingertip, its dark circle the only dark point on that pale rib, with a faint ring of reflected light at its rim. It is mounted on the RIB, never floating on the glass.
IT MUST BE PLANTED WITHOUT BEING ANNOUNCED. In the panels before she finds it, the bead is genuinely present and genuinely visible in the dome — but it is NOT emphasised in any way: do not light it, do not make it glow, do not centre the composition on it, do not enlarge it, do not add a red indicator. It is one small dark point among the dome's ordinary fittings, easy to miss on a first look and unmistakable on a second. From the panel where she notices it onward, it stays exactly the same size and in exactly the same seam — the shot sizes change, the lens never does.

SCREEN DIRECTION — holds across the whole sheet: the dome and its lens are ABOVE her, so her attention travels UP; when she turns away from it she turns toward frame-LEFT and the bed; when she turns back she turns toward frame-RIGHT and looks up. Key light is the cold blue-white dome light from above. Never mirror or flip a panel. Only a panel marked NEUTRAL may reset this.

CAMERA KIT — every panel names its setup. A setup fixes the camera's side of the glass, its height and its distance; the action inside it progresses from panel to panel. Nyla moves within this one location: the fixed-camera rule applies to each setup, not to her body.
A — WS, camera INSIDE the pod at bed height, wide enough to hold Nyla standing and the dome ceiling curving above her, the furniture in its locked positions across the frame.
B — MCU, camera INSIDE the pod at her eye height, square on her, she occupies the middle third; the curved pale dome soft behind and above her head.
C — LOW MS, camera INSIDE the pod near the floor tilted UP: Nyla's head and shoulders across the lower third, the dome ceiling filling the upper two-thirds with its glass panes, pale ribs and fittings, the lens bead among them on a rib.
D — INSERT MACRO, camera INSIDE the pod, macro-close on the dome rib itself, no figure in frame at all.
E — LENS POV, NEUTRAL: the camera occupies the LENS'S OWN POSITION, set in the dome's rib hub directly above the centre of the pod, looking straight DOWN into the room. Nyla is below, small-to-medium in frame and centred, foreshortened by the height, her face tilted up. What surrounds her in this view is the pod FLOOR — the circular mat, the coffee table and its glowing sphere lamp, the bed and armchair splayed outward around her — never the dome, which is where this camera is. A faint barrel distortion at the frame edges, like a security lens. This is the only panel that may break the screen-direction lock.
F — MS FROM BESIDE THE BED, camera INSIDE the pod at her shoulder height, level and square, holding her whole upper body in the middle third with the bed at frame-LEFT and the dome curving above.
G — WS, camera OUTSIDE the pod at bed height, shooting in through the curved pane: the whole sphere in frame with the dark warren stacks around it, the closed human-sized hatch visible in its frame-LEFT curve with the catwalk running past its sill, Nyla small inside.

DRAW THESE PANELS IN ORDER:

[1] POD · WS · (SETUP A) — Nyla stands alone near the centre of the pod, arms loose at her sides, chest still settling from breath, occupying about a fifth of the frame; the furniture holds its locked positions around her and the pale dome curves overhead, its ribs and small flush fittings ordinary and unremarked, the warren's faint glow visible through the glass between them — the dark bead is present on one rib near the hub directly above the pod's centre, no larger and no brighter than the fittings beside it. Her face is three-quarter to the lens, eyes level and unfocused. Nothing in the composition points at the dome.

[2] POD · LOW MS · (SETUP C) — the first notice: Nyla's head and shoulders across the lower third, face tilted UP and three-quarter to the lens, eyes narrowing as they find something; the dome fills the upper two-thirds and the small dark bead sits on one of its pale ribs, on the same rib and at the same size as panel 1, still unlit and unemphasised — it is simply now in the same frame as her gaze.

[3] POD · INSERT MACRO · (SETUP D) — the lens itself, no figure in frame: a dark glass bead about the size of a fingertip, set flush into a pale white structural rib of the dome, a faint ring of reflected light at its rim, a little dust caught along the rib beside it, clear glass panes falling away dark on either side. Cold, still, patient. Nothing else in the panel.

[4] POD · MCU · (SETUP B) — the instinct: Nyla square in the middle third at her eye height, three-quarter to the lens, eyes wide and breath caught, her near shoulder already lifting toward the dome as if to put something between herself and it; the pale dome soft behind her head.

[5] POD · MS · (SETUP F) — the instinct completed: Nyla has turned toward frame-LEFT and the bed, her BACK now to camera and to the dome, face not visible, head lowered between her shoulders, one hand half-raised near her collarbone; her feet stay where they were and she has not walked away — only her upper body has turned. The bed sits at frame-LEFT, the dome curves above, the bead still on its rib.

[6] POD · MCU · (SETUP B) — the same camera as panel 4, the moment progressed: she has stopped and come back square to the room, three-quarter to the lens again, eyes dropping once and then lifting, jaw setting — the decision landing in her face and nowhere else. Her shoulder has come down. The pale dome soft behind her.

[7] POD · LOW MS · (SETUP C) — the same camera as panel 2, the moment progressed: Nyla has straightened to her full height with her chin lifted, head and shoulders across the lower third, face tilted UP and now nearly to-lens, looking directly at the bead; the dome fills the upper two-thirds, the bead unchanged on its rib. She is not hiding.

[8] LENS POV · NEUTRAL · (SETUP E) — the pivot of the film, seen from the lens's own position in the dome looking straight down: Nyla stands centred below, foreshortened, her face tilted fully up and SQUARE TO THE LENS with her eyes looking directly into camera, no flinch and no three-quarter — she is looking back. Around and beneath her the pod floor splays outward: the circular mat, the coffee table with its glowing sphere lamp, the bed at one side and the armchair at the other. Faint barrel distortion at the frame edges like a security feed. The dome is NOT visible in this panel, because this camera is in it.

[9] POD · WS FROM OUTSIDE · (SETUP G) — from the catwalk outside, shooting in through the curved pane: the whole sphere sits among the dark stacks of other glowing pods, the closed hatch in its frame-LEFT curve with the catwalk running past the sill, and Nyla is small inside, standing at the centre with her face still tilted up toward the dome, holding the look. Her face is not readable at this size — her posture is. The warren stretches away into darkness around her.

CONSTRAINTS: one action per panel; never mirror or flip a panel; exactly one person in every panel and no other figures anywhere; never swap which side of the frame the bed, armchair, desk or hatch occupies; the lens bead stays the same size, the same darkness and in the same dome rib in every panel that shows it, and is never lit, never glowing, never centred and never enlarged before she notices it; when Nyla turns, only her upper body turns unless a panel says she walks — her feet and the furniture never re-stage; the dome is visible in every interior panel except the macro insert and the lens-POV panel; in the lens-POV panel the dome is never visible because the camera is in it; keep every panel free of readable text except its number label.
