TRANSITION PLAN — worked example, the eight-scene structure of the bubble-pod film

This is what a film-level boundary pass produces, and what L23 requires before any
sheet is boarded. It is an EXAMPLE of the artifact, not a law; the laws are L23-L26
in BOARD-LAWS.md. Scene numbering follows Ryan's split ruling: the original opening
scene becomes three (escape / run and dead end / return).

SCENES
  1a  The escape          — pod interior, then out through the hatch
  1b  The run             — the warren corridor, ending at its dead end
  1c  The return          — back along the corridor, back inside, hatch shut
  2   The audience        — the elite viewing hall, watching and laughing
  3   The discovery       — pod interior, finds the lens, looks into it
  4   The performance     — pod interior, performs to the lens
  5   The reaction        — the hall, the elites lean forward
  6   The close           — the screen holds her smile, one pod burns brighter

---

BOUNDARY 1a → 1b        RELATIONSHIP: CONTINUATION
  OUT (last panel of 1a): from the corridor at chest height, she climbs through the
    open hatch and plants one foot on the catwalk, body already turning frame-RIGHT.
  IN (first panel of 1b): low on the wet catwalk, she is already running, entering
    from frame-LEFT and driving frame-RIGHT down the tunnel.
  CARRIES: the subject and her screen direction. She exits frame-right, so she
    enters from frame-left and keeps travelling the same way (L25).

BOUNDARY 1b → 1c        RELATIONSHIP: CONTINUATION, with the reversal on screen
  OUT (last panel of 1b): the corridor's dead end fills the frame — more pods, no
    door, no stair. She has stopped, and IN THIS PANEL she turns: shoulders coming
    round, face coming to three-quarter, her drive direction breaking.
  IN (first panel of 1c): she walks frame-LEFT back down the same corridor, small,
    the vanishing point now behind her.
  CARRIES: the subject. The direction reverses, so the turn itself is the OUT shot
    rather than an unexplained flip (L25).

BOUNDARY 1c → 2         RELATIONSHIP: NESTED HANDOFF + CONTRAST
  OUT (last panel of 1c): from outside, the sphere sealed again among thousands of
    identical glowing pods, she small inside it, the hatch closed.
  IN (first panel of 2): that same grid of pods, framed identically — then revealed
    as the feed on a building-sized screen.
  CARRIES: the image itself, repeated exactly (L11), then its context. The contrast
    is scale: one sealed room becomes wallpaper on someone else's wall.

BOUNDARY 2 → 3          RELATIONSHIP: SIGHTLINE BRIDGE
  OUT (last panel of 2): from the screen's own position, looking back at the row of
    elites laughing — the camera is standing where the pods are.
  IN (first panel of 3): her pod interior, the ceiling in frame, the lens present
    but unremarked (L18).
  CARRIES: the camera's position becomes her position. We were looking at them from
    where she lives; now we are in the room with her, and the thing they were
    watching through is in the frame.

BOUNDARY 3 → 4          RELATIONSHIP: MATCH
  OUT (last panel of 3): the lens POV, looking straight down, her face tilted up and
    square to the lens, eyes into camera, not moving.
  IN (first panel of 4): the identical lens POV, the identical framing — and she has
    begun to move: standing straighter, smoothing her hair, starting to play to it.
  CARRIES: the exact frame. Same shot, new intention — the cut is the change of
    behaviour, not the change of picture. Note this is a deliberate exact match,
    which L26 permits, and NOT the near-repeat it forbids.

BOUNDARY 4 → 5          RELATIONSHIP: NESTED HANDOFF
  OUT (last panel of 4): the lens POV, her performing directly into it.
  IN (first panel of 5): that exact view, alone, filling the frame — then the pull
    out revealing it as the hall's screen with five elites beneath it.
  CARRIES: the frame, repeated exactly, then its context. (Boarded and approved.)

BOUNDARY 5 → 6          RELATIONSHIP: CONTRAST
  OUT (last panel of 5): tight on one elite's face, awake and interested for the
    first time in years.
  IN (first panel of 6): the screen holding her smile, huge, filling everything.
  CARRIES: the axis of opposition is scale and power — the smallest possible frame
    of the watchers, cut against the largest possible frame of the watched.

---

WHAT THIS ARTIFACT IS FOR

Every OUT description above becomes the OUTGOING block in one scene's board prompt,
and every IN description becomes the INCOMING block of the next. That is the only
way a sheet can know how to begin and end, because the planner sees one scene at a
time.

Two consequences worth noting:
1. A scene's first and last panels are NOT free choices. They are assigned by the
   boundary pass. The scene chooses its middle.
2. Two adjacent scenes can require the SAME frame (boundaries 3-4 and 4-5 both do).
   That is legal and deliberate; what is illegal is two frames that are almost the
   same (L26).
