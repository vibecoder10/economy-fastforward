# StoryEngine flagship Custom Film — creator runbook and creative brief

## The exact first-user path

1. Sign in to StoryEngine and go to **Home**. The first screen is the chat composer headed **“What should we make?”**
2. Paste the flagship request below and send it:

   > Make me the ultimate cinematic five-minute Custom Film about the day the internet went dark. Use exactly four acts: a 45-second thriller opening; a 105-second investigation through a fictional outage map, evidence board, and incident timeline; a 90-second bilingual witness sequence where Mara reveals a hidden recovery key through a radio transmission; and a 60-second four-node network explanation, city reconnection, and StoryEngine product reveal. Keep the same evolving turquoise signal pulse across all four acts. Keep all important faces, evidence, captions, and product UI crop-safe for 9:16 excerpts. Use my connected accounts only and do not exceed a $15 hard spending cap.

3. StoryEngine returns a creator-safe plan. No footage has been generated and nothing has been charged at this point.
4. Review the **Custom Film production blueprint**. The canonical treatment is expected to show:
   - four ordered acts;
   - exact act durations adding to 5:00;
   - images, motion clips, and voice tracks per act;
   - a **Director's shot blueprint** grouped under those acts, where every beat names its approved media treatment and the simultaneous visual systems, transformation, camera, captions, and sound behavior;
   - section estimates and the $5.57 total estimate;
   - the $15 hard ceiling and $9.43 unused headroom;
   - the note that Remotion finishing adds no provider spend.
5. Read the shot blueprint as the exact creative promise, not as separate technology sections. A beat may combine approved footage or animated imagery with the pulse, map/evidence/timeline/waveform/network/product graphics, camera movement, bilingual captions, transition state, and audio behavior at the same time. Confirm the sequence feels like one film and that the named transformation carries visual ideas between beats.
6. The prompt is handled by the general Custom Film planner, so do not assume the expected treatment was reproduced merely because the request was accepted. Compare all four durations, media counts, layered beat directions, and the quote with the table below. If anything differs—or any story or creative choice is wrong—click **Keep editing** and describe the correction in ordinary language. Any edit clears the previous approval and produces a newly bound plan and quote.
7. Only when the blueprint is right, click **Approve paid production · up to $15.00**. This is the action that authorizes paid BYOK generation. StoryEngine must execute the approved beat recipes rather than replanning them after approval.
8. StoryEngine creates the video and shows **Building your video…** with an **Open it** button. Open it to enter the production workspace.
9. The production map shows **Custom Film**, the ordered four-section mix, and live pipeline stages. Custom Film remains one film; Remotion is an internal finishing engine, not a fifth public style or provider choice.
10. Review generated section media before final assembly. Reject identity drift, weak evidence framing, unreadable text, awkward crops, or generic footage before accepting a section.
11. After assembly, the card changes to **Your video is ready to review 🎬**. Click **Review it**, watch the full master with sound, then inspect the 4:45–5:00 product reveal and crop-aware excerpts before any upload or release.

## Creative direction — what to improve over the synthetic engineering reference

### One film, not four demos

The turquoise pulse is the film’s visual sentence. It begins as a distant signal, becomes the map route and evidence connector, resolves into Mara’s waveform/recovery key, then becomes the reconnecting network and final StoryEngine master line. Its rhythm and brightness should evolve with the story rather than reset at section boundaries.

### Act 1 — cinematic outage opening, 0:00–0:45

- Begin on human-scale normality, not a generic skyline montage.
- Use five coherent night images from one fictional city and preserve geographic/weather continuity.
- Let power and network loss propagate through practical details: traffic lights, payment terminal, apartment windows, radio static.
- Keep the title minimal and centered inside the safe area.
- Audio should move from city texture to an intentional silence drop, followed by the first turquoise pulse.

### Act 2 — investigation, 0:45–2:30

- Make the fictional map readable in under two seconds: one route, three evidence pins, one spreading outage state.
- Evidence must feel authored, not like loose stock cards. Use clear document hierarchy, restrained redaction, short labels, and connector lines that explain causality.
- Preserve Ryan’s accepted repair: **Evidence Board** stays centered inside its lower boundary.
- The timeline should earn each new date/counter and connect back to the map rather than becoming a separate infographic.
- Keep faces and decisive evidence out of overlay collision zones.

### Act 3 — Mara witness/radio sequence, 2:30–4:00

- Generate and lock one Mara reference before motion so identity, clothing, room, and radio remain consistent.
- Frame Mara center-safe with negative space for captions; do not place evidence overlays over her face or hands.
- Spanish and English lines need distinct speaker/translation hierarchy, Unicode-safe typography, and measured word emphasis.
- Transform the waveform into the recovery key on the same pulse cadence established in Acts 1–2.
- Use the film’s strongest silence moment immediately before the hidden-code reveal.

### Act 4 — explanation, recovery, and product reveal, 4:00–5:00

- Explain the failure with only four nodes and one ordered reconnection at a time.
- Let the city recovery reuse locations from the opening so the resolution feels causal rather than decorative.
- Reserve 4:45–5:00 for the clearest product story:
  1. the original chat request;
  2. the approved four-act blueprint;
  3. media, voice, caption, motion, and audio production lanes;
  4. all lanes converging into one master line;
  5. StoryEngine wordmark and finished-film frame.
- Product UI must be based on the actual StoryEngine experience, not a fantasy dashboard. Crop-safe request, plan, lane labels, brand, and master line must all remain legible in the centered 9:16 excerpt.

## Acceptance before any public release

- Watch the entire master at normal speed with sound.
- Inspect exact act transitions and every motion primitive at its intended moment.
- Confirm Mara identity, bilingual caption timing, speech clarity, music ducking, silence drop, and no accidental silence/clipping.
- Inspect representative 16:9 frames and centered 9:16 crops from every act.
- Inspect every second from 4:45–5:00 and the final frame.
- Confirm actual duration, 1920×1080 resolution, 24 fps, audio, captions, storage readback, and final artifact identity.
- Do not upload, publish, or spend on rerolls without a separate decision after review.
