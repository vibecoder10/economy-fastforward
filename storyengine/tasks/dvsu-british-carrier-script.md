## 1. I49 HMS Argus — 99 words

HMS Argus (I49) was a British aircraft carrier with a displacement of 14,700 tons, an overall length of 565.0 ft, and a beam of 68.0 ft. Argus evaluated various types of arresting gear, general procedures needed to operate a number of aircraft in concert and fleet tactics. Argus was recommissioned and partially modernised shortly before the Second World War and served as a training ship for deck-landing practice until June 1940. By 1942, the Royal Navy was very short of aircraft carriers, and Argus was pressed into front-line service despite her lack of speed and armament.

## 2. 47 HMS Furious — 104 words

HMS Furious (47) was a modified Courageous-class battlecruiser launched in 1916. She was modified to become an aircraft carrier while under construction, with her forward turret removed and a flying-off deck added. She was converted to a flush-deck aircraft carrier between 1921 and 1925. After her conversion, Furious was used extensively for trials of naval aircraft, and later as a training ship once large, modern fleet carriers such as Ark Royal entered service in the 1930s. By the outbreak of war in 1939, Furious was serving as a deck landing training carrier. She served with both the British Home Fleet and the Mediterranean Fleet.

## 3. I36 HMS Vindictive — 110 words

HMS Vindictive was originally laid down as the Hawkins-class cruiser HMS Cavendish, ordered in April 1916 and built by Harland & Wolff in Belfast. In 1917, the navy decided they needed more aircraft carriers, and she was redesigned as an experimental cruiser-carrier with a split deck design. She was renamed HMS Vindictive in June 1918 to commemorate the old cruiser used as a blockship at Zeebrugge and Ostend. Launched in January 1918, she was commissioned on 1 October 1918 and joined the Grand Fleet just weeks before World War I ended. She could carry 6–12 aircraft and was reclassified as an aircraft carrier before returning to cruiser configuration in 1924.

## 4. 94 HMS Eagle (1918) — 100 words

HMS Eagle began as a Chilean battleship purchased by the Royal Navy on 28 February 1918 and converted into an aircraft carrier. She was launched on 8 June 1918. Eagle retained the battleship's mixture of coal and fuel oil, with quantities increased to 3,200 long tons of coal and 1,750 long tons of oil. Eagle spent the first nine months of World War II in the Indian Ocean searching for German commerce raiders. The German submarine U-73 torpedoed and sank Eagle on 11 August 1942 with 131 lives lost while escorting a convoy to Malta during Operation Pedestal.

Sources: [1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Eagle_(1918)), [2](https://naval-history.net/xGM-Chrono-04CV-Eagle.htm)

## 5. 95 HMS Hermes (1924) — 100 words

HMS Hermes was designed from the keel up to be an aircraft carrier. Like the Japanese carrier Hōshō, she was based on a cruiser-type hull and was initially designed to carry both wheeled aircraft and seaplanes. Her cruiser-derived form limited aircraft capacity, typically numbering 12 fighters. During sea trials, her turbines produced 41,318 shp, giving Hermes a speed of 26.2 knots. Finally commissioned in 1924, Hermes served briefly with the Atlantic Fleet before spending the bulk of her career assigned to the Mediterranean Fleet and the China Station. Hermes pioneered carrier anti-piracy operations while on the China Station.

## 6. Courageous class — 99 words

Courageous class ships were originally built as light battlecruisers intended for an Operation in the Baltic in 1917 which never materialized. The first two ships, Courageous and Glorious, were commissioned in 1917 and spent the war patrolling the North Sea. All three ships were laid up after the war, but were rebuilt into the Courageous-class aircraft carriers during the 1920s. Courageous and Glorious could carry 48 aircraft, compared with 36 carried by their half-sister Furious on about the same displacement. Glorious and Courageous were sunk early in the Second World War and Furious was sold for scrap in 1948.

Sources: [1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Courageous-class_battlecruiser), [2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Courageous_(50)), [3](https://www.naval-encyclopedia.com/ww1/uk/courageous-class-battlecruisers.php)

## 7. 91 Ark Royal (1938) — 102 words

HMS Ark Royal (91) was an aircraft carrier of the Royal Navy, built by Cammell Laird at Birkenhead and completed in November 1938. Designed in 1934 to fit the restrictions of the Washington Naval Treaty, the ship was the first in which the hangars and flight deck were an integral part of the hull, instead of an add-on or part of the superstructure. She was designed to carry 72 aircraft, but the actual number carried was between 50 and 60 as aircraft grew larger during construction. Ark Royal operated in some of the most active naval theatres of the Second World War.

Sources: [1](https://baike.baidu.com/en/item/HMS%20Ark%20Royal%20II/1490319), [2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Ark_Royal_(91))

## 8. D10 HMS Audacity — 94 words

HMS Audacity (D10) was originally built as the German merchant ship Hannover, captured in the West Indies in March 1940. She was converted and commissioned as HMS Empire Audacity, then as HMS Audacity, for convoy defence in the Western Approaches. The ship was a flush deck carrier with no island structure or hangar deck. She was fitted with Type 79B air warning radar. Her Martlets shot down two Condors during operations, and U-131 was attacked on 17 December; unable to dive after the attack, U-131 was scuttled by her crew, who were taken prisoner.

## 9. D94 HMS Activity — 85 words

HMS Activity (D94) was an escort carrier that served with the Royal Navy during the Second World War. Originally laid down as a refrigerated cargo ship, she was converted for use as an escort aircraft carrier during build at Caledon shipyards in Dundee. She was the second British-built warship of this type, following HMS Audacity. After her refit, Activity took part in convoy escort duties in the North Atlantic. Until August 1944, her aircraft participated in the sinking of two U-boats and damaged three others.

## 10. Illustrious class — 93 words

Illustrious class was a class of Royal Navy aircraft carriers laid down in the late 1930s as part of British rearmament in response to the emerging threats of Nazi Germany, Fascist Italy and Imperial Japan. The class comprised four vessels: HM Ships Illustrious, Formidable, Victorious and Indomitable. Designed within the restrictions of the Second London Naval Treaty, which limited carrier size to an upper limit of 23,000 tons, the class relied on anti-aircraft armament and the passive defence provided by an armoured flight deck for survival, resulting in a reduced aircraft complement.

Sources: [1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illustrious-class_aircraft_carrier), [2](https://www.seaforces.org/marint/Royal-Navy/Aircraft-Carrier/Illustrious-class.htm)

## 11. Nairana class — 103 words

Nairana class escort carriers were a class of three escort carriers built for the Royal Navy during the Second World War. Escort carriers were designed to protect convoys of merchant ships from U-boat and aircraft attack. In July 1942 the Admiralty requisitioned three merchant hulls while still on the stocks for completion as Nairana class escort carriers. Two of the ships chosen were sisters, fast cargo-passenger ships being built for the Port Line Ltd; the Port Pirie by John Brown on the Clyde was to become HMS Nairana, and the Port Sydney at Swan Hunter on the Tyne was to become HMS Vindex.

Sources: [1](https://en-academic.com/dic.nsf/enwiki/5269332), [2](https://www.royalnavyresearcharchive.org.uk/ESCORT/NAIRANA.htm)

## 12. HMS Campania (D48) — 87 words

HMS Campania (D48) was an Escort Aircraft Carrier of the Royal Navy. The ship was one of a pair of passenger cargo vessels of 12.000 tons ordered by the Shaw Savill Line in 1940 from Harland & Wolff, Belfast. The incomplete mercantile hull was requisitioned by the Admiralty in 29th July 1942 and adapted for use as an Escort Aircraft Carrier by Harland and Wolff of Belfast. She was commissioned 7 Mar 1944. Campania operated escorting convoys and doing anti-submarine work in the Atlantic and Arctic theatres.

## 13. F61 HMS Pretoria Castle — 99 words

HMS Pretoria Castle (F61) was a Union-Castle ocean liner built at Harland & Wolff shipyards in Belfast and launched in 1938. She was requisitioned by the Royal Navy in October 1939 and converted to an armed merchant cruiser with 6-inch and 3-inch guns, entering service in November 1939. By July 1942 the Admiralty purchased her outright for conversion to an escort carrier, a process that took more than a year. She was used for flight training and to test aircraft models assigned to fleet carriers. After the war she was converted back into a passenger liner and renamed Warwick Castle.

## 14. Implacable class — 101 words

Implacable class aircraft carriers were built for the Royal Navy during World War II as an improved version of the Illustrious-class aircraft carriers for the 1938 Naval Programme. Designed to stow 48 aircraft in their hangars, the use of a permanent deck park allowed the Implacable class to accommodate up to 81 aircraft. The ships were equipped with two lifts on the centreline, with the aft lift serving both hangars. HMS Implacable was engaged in operations against German ships in Norway and was transferred to the Pacific afterward. Implacable returned home in 1946 and became the Home Fleet's deck-landing training carrier.

Sources: [1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Implacable_(R86)), [2](https://wiki.worldofwarships.com/Ship:Implacable)

## 15. I72 HMS Unicorn — 107 words

HMS Unicorn (I72) was a unique light aircraft carrier built for the Royal Navy during World War II. Ordered on 14 April 1939 and laid down at Harland and Wolff in Belfast on 29 June, she was designed to meet afloat support requirements for sustained operations by armoured fleet carriers. She had an overall length of 640 feet, a beam of 90 feet 3 inches, and could carry about 36 operational aircraft. Unicorn was designed with a full-length, 600-foot-long flight deck with arresting gear. She proved the value of the concept, leading to two similar support ships, Perseus and Pioneer, being converted from light carriers under construction.

Sources: [1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Unicorn_(I72)), [2](https://www.royalnavyresearcharchive.org.uk/BPF-EIF/Ships/UNICORN.htm)

## 16. Colossus class — 48 words

Colossus class light fleet carriers were ordered under a War Emergency Build Programme. The ships displaced 18000 tons at full load and measured 212 meters overall with a maximum speed of 25 knots. Class leader Colossus was transferred to the French Navy in August 1946 and renamed Arromanches.

## 17. Majestic class — 96 words

Majestic class aircraft carriers were the second batch of Light Fleet Aircraft Carriers, completed to a modified design and therefore classified as a separate class. The final six hulls were modified during construction to handle larger and faster aircraft. The catapult, arrester gear, and lifts were upgraded to enable the ships to operate heavier aircraft, and the class featured a strengthened flight deck. The Majestic class had the same dimensions as the Colossus class but was 1,500 tons heavier. When the Second World War ended, none of the ships were complete and construction was suspended.

## 18. Audacious class — 95 words

Audacious class aircraft carriers were conceived in wartime but came of age in the Cold War. The class was drawn up with a focus on size, power and adaptability, and the carriers were built to accommodate both piston-engined and jet aircraft. Eventually, only Ark Royal and Audacious (renamed Eagle) would be completed. HMS Ark Royal was commissioned in 1955 and served until 1979, by which time she was the last remaining conventional catapult-and-arrested-landing aircraft carrier in the Royal Navy. Ark Royal was also the only non-American vessel to operate the McDonnell Douglas F-4 Phantom II.

## 19. Centaur class — 89 words

Centaur class aircraft carriers were the last of the Royal Navy's light fleet carrier designs started during the closing years of World War II. Conceived in 1943 as an improvement on the earlier Majestic and Colossus designs, the class was intended to handle heavier and faster aircraft of the future. The ships displaced 28700 tons at full load and featured a full flight deck and hangar for up to 42 aircraft. Centaur was the first to be completed and was partially modernised with steam catapults shortly after being commissioned.

## 20. Invincible class — 108 words

The Invincible-class light aircraft carriers were the capital ships of the Royal Navy during the latter half of the Cold War and the post-Cold War era. The class was designed as anti-deep-sea vessel command ships for NATO task forces in the Atlantic, carrying a heavy air group of Sea King ASW helicopters and Sea Harrier V/STOL jets. The design featured a continuous flight deck with a distinctive island superstructure on the starboard side. A major architectural feature was the ski-jump ramp at the bow, which curved upward to enhance Sea Harrier payload and takeoff performance. HMS Invincible was deployed in the Yugoslav Wars and the Iraq War.

Sources: [1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Invincible_(R05)), [2](https://www.globalsecurity.org/military/world/europe/invincible.htm)

## 21. Queen Elizabeth class — 95 words

Queen Elizabeth class carriers are among the largest and most powerful warships ever built for the Royal Navy. HMS Queen Elizabeth (R08) is the lead ship, designed to restore fixed-wing carrier aviation to British naval service. At 284 metres long with a flight deck approximately 70 metres across at its widest point, the Queen Elizabeth class is considerably larger than any previous Royal Navy warship. The ships are capable of carrying 60 aircraft including fixed wing, rotary wing and autonomous vehicles. HMS Queen Elizabeth was commissioned on 7 December 2017 and entered service in 2020.
