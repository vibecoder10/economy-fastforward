# DvsU Quality Law

**Status: draft for Ryan's one-pass approval. Source of authority: Ryan's ruling 2026-07-16 - Anton's 14 scripts are the quality-gate law for the DvsU engine.**

These laws are reverse-engineered from Anton's 14 shipped scripts (369 entries, roughly 40,000 words) plus the DvsU pipeline docs. They are the standard the engine is graded against.

**The bar is same-or-higher, not indistinguishable.** The engine's job is to clear Anton's quality floor or beat it, measured by these laws in front of a 55+ audience that fact-checks everything. The goal is not output indistinguishable from Anton, and not a forgery of one writer's fingerprint. A law is a floor to clear, not a style to copy. Where Anton varies by register (tight-production vs naval spec-block vs crew-testimony), the law names the register and its band rather than a single number.

## Corpus coverage and verification

- 12 of 14 scripts have full per-entry analysis tables; two readers (US Military
  Helicopter, US Aircraft Carrier) overflowed and their entry-level twist/punch
  tables are missing (backfillable; does not change any law below).
- The word-count distribution table was INDEPENDENTLY RE-DERIVED by code from the
  raw text of all 14 scripts and matches the analysts' numbers exactly:
  pooled n=368, median 107, p25 95, p90 131, under-80 4 percent, under-90 17 percent.
  Per-script medians: destroyers 85, helicopters 85, britishCV 90, usCV 97,
  bombers 103, neverbuilt 106, usBB 107, hatedwarships 108, sovietsubs 111,
  hatedjets 112, britishBB 116, sunkCV 119, hatedhelis 128, landingships 134.
  A 90 floor rejects the MEDIAN entry of two Anton scripts; 80 passes 96 percent.

## How to read this

- **Every law is triangle-wired** (Ryan's contract-triangle law): it must live in the **build prompt** (tell the generator), the **repair warning** (flag it on self-review), and the **gate** (test it at the boundary), landed in the same commit. Each row shows all three legs as **B / R / G**.
- **Severity:**
  - **hard-gate** = block or reject; output cannot ship if it fails.
  - **warn** = flag for review; ships only if the deviation is deliberate.
  - **guidance** = a prior that shapes generation; no reject.
- **Section 3** lists every law where today's code or docs differ from the law above, with the one-line fix.
- **Section 4** lists only the calls that still need Ryan.

### Writing craft

| ID | Law (testable) | Evidence (counts) | Triangle - B / R / G | Sev |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| QL-1 | Reject under 80 spoken words; warn 80-95; reject over 170. Hit the register median, not a fixed number: tight-production 85-105, spec-block naval 100-120, long-form (crew/sunk/never-built) 110-130; the folded-outro final entry may reach 160. | 369 entries: median 107, p25 95, p90 131; under 80 = 4% (15), under 90 = 17% (64). R flag under-80 (thicken or fold) and warn-band (confirm terse on purpose). G reject under 80, warn 80-95, reject over 170. | hard-gate |
| QL-2 | Word count tracks narrative weight and position, not a flat target. First entry runs at or above median (never the shortest); final entry runs plus 10 to 30 (folds the bookend); marquee machines 110-150; bare prototypes and connective entries 80-90. Thin on an important machine is a defect; thin on a bare prototype is correct. | First-entry median 107 (min 91). Last-minus-mid median +10 (8/14 scripts +5 to +36). Marquee: Iowa 134, Hood 131, Vanguard 152. Bare: New Mexico BB 58, Glorious-CV 74, YB-60 81. | B weight the budget by importance and position. R important-machine-thin, first-entry-shortest, or final entry with no bookend. G flag a below-median count on a high-importance machine, and a final entry carrying no bookend. | guidance |
| QL-3 | Every entry runs on a designed-vs-used gap (built for X, used as Y). Absent only when the machine was used exactly as designed - and then it MUST substitute a superlative, a legacy/lineage hook, timing-irony, or an anti-twist payload. Absent gap with no substitute is the worst entry in the corpus. | Present 93/104 labeled entries (89%). All 11 absent cases are purpose-built-used-as-designed or pure connective; the one absent-and-unsubstituted case (New Mexico BB, 58w) is its script's only summary close. | B state the designed intent then invert to actual use, or substitute a payload. R "no gap and no substitute - reads as a spec dump." G reject entries with neither a twist nor a substitute. | hard-gate |
| QL-4 | Name the twist from an expanded menu; do not default to "other." The five canonical types cover about half the corpus; add the recurring subtypes: lost-fly-off, treaty/politics-cancelled, cost-killed, mission-obsoleted-by-countermeasure, redundant-backup, incremental-stopgap, peaked-obsolete-concept, killed-by-secondary-threat, self-destruction, role-discontinued-by-budget, built-for-a-threat-that-never-existed. | 105 labeled entries: other 46%, role-change 18%, obsolete-but-enduring 11%, absent 11%, myth-vs-reality 8%, cheap-beats-good 4%, ambition-outran-tech 3%. British BB tags 24/28 (86%) "other" for lack of a bucket. | B offer the expanded menu, pick the closest named subtype. R "tagged other - is there a named subtype it actually is?" G warn if over 40% of a script falls to "other." | guidance |
| QL-5 | Every entry ends on a sharpened verdict that turns the facts into a lesson. Legal forms: single-hammer, antithesis, concede-then-cut, triad. Summary and recap closers are banned. | 105 entries: single-hammer 41%, antithesis 25%, concede-then-cut 22%, triad 10%, summary (BAD) 2% - both summaries are the flagged-weakest entry in their script. British BB and DvsU-Helicopter land zero summaries. | B close on one of the four forms; elevate, do not recap. R "closer restates facts - rewrite as an antithesis or a single hammer." G reject a closer with no turn and no elevation. | hard-gate |
| QL-6 | Reach first for the house punch - a two-part parallel antithesis that restates the designed-vs-used gap and lands on the result side ("X did A. Y did B."), each half 4-9 words. Single-hammer, concede-then-cut, triad are legal fallbacks. | DvsU-Helicopter lands nearly every punch this way ("They ordered an ambulance. They got an air force."). Spec-block scripts use it too (bomber 8/23, US BB 6/27). | B draft the punch as a two-sentence antithesis first. R "one clause - can it split into a designed/used antithesis?" G guidance only; never reject another legal form. | guidance |
| QL-7 | Cap bare name-openers ("The [Maker] [Designation]...") at about 57% (17 of 30). At least 40% of entries open on a bridge, a role/thesis claim, date/era context, or a count-fragment (crew register: a cold symptom or testimony). No 3 identical opener types in a row. | 105 spec-block entries: name 57%, bridge 24%, role/thesis 10%, date 6%, other 4%. Crew variant flips to about 0 name-openers; the bomber's one 4-bridge run is a deliberate run-off marker. | B vary the opener; spend the name budget, do not blow it. R "Nth name-opener in a row, or name-openers over 57%." G warn if name-openers exceed 60% or more than 2 identical opener types run consecutively. | warn |
| QL-8 | Chain to the prior machine about 40% of the time, and cluster the bridges (chronology, within-class sequences, competition run-offs, shared eras) rather than spread them evenly. Never zero, never every entry. Plant and pay off at least one long-arc callback. | has_bridge pooled about 40% (bomber 17%, US BB 48%, British CV 67%). Long-arc: YB-49 plants "Northrop would return with the B-2 forty years later," paid off in the B-2 entry. | B chain rivals, sequences, and eras; plant one or more callbacks and pay them off. R "unchained run inside a serial sub-sequence." G warn if a script has 0 bridges or over 75% chained. | guidance |
| QL-9 | Every entry carries at least one sticky fact beyond the spec skeleton (nickname, named record, marquee act, shocking stat, distinctive feature, superlative, or crew quote). Exceptions only for deliberately-bare loser or connective entries. | 99/105 (94%) carry one; the 6 without are the barest entries (XB-15, XB-46/YB-60 loser-prototypes, pure-argument, New Mexico connective). | B land one memorable fact per entry. R "pure spec plus fate, no sticky fact - add the nickname/record/act." G warn on any entry with no memorable fact unless it is tagged deliberately-bare. | warn |
| QL-10 | Spell numbers a narrator would speak (counts, calibers, knots, tonnages, durations, percentages, large round figures). Digits only for alphanumeric designations, calendar years/dates, and very large exact figures (casualty tolls like 1,177, costs, hull numbers like BB-66). | DvsU-Helicopter spells all but designations and years (spelled per 1k = 45, corpus max). US BB spells 17/17 calibers. Digit-camp outliers (US Destroyer, British Carrier) need normalizing. Casualty tolls stay digits even in spell-scripts. | B spell counts and measures; digits for designations, years, and exact tolls. R "measure in digits (30 knots) - spell it (thirty knots)." G flag digit measures and small-counts; never flag a digit designation, year, or exact toll. | warn |
| QL-11 | Alphanumeric designations are ALWAYS digits, never spelled (B-17, BB-66, F-35B, CH-47, Mi-8, I-400, U-29, Type XXI). Zero exceptions. | 0 spelled designations in 40,000 words; the bomber alone carries 166 designation tokens, every one a digit. | B keep every designation in canonical digit form verbatim. R "a designation got spelled or reformatted - restore the digit form." G hard-reject a spelled designation. | hard-gate |
| QL-12 | Ban thumbnail-hype adjectives (incredible, amazing, stunning, insane, epic, jaw-dropping, mind-blowing, game-changing, breathtaking, unbelievable, spectacular). Superlatives allowed ONLY when anchored to a verifiable fact. Avoid free-floating emotive nouns (nightmare, death-trap, killer). | 0 banned-list occurrences in 40,000 words. Emotive nouns (nightmare 7, death-trap 2, killer 2) confined to the older, weaker MostHated-Warships. Anton's superlatives are fact-anchored ("most-produced... 18,482 built"). | B no hype; earn every superlative with the number that proves it. R "hype word or unanchored superlative - cut it or attach the fact." G hard-reject any banned token; warn on an unanchored superlative or a free-floating emotive noun. | hard-gate |
| QL-13 | Short sentences (6 words or fewer) are a tool concentrated at the verdict-punch and act-break, not spread evenly (about 20% pooled share). Match the register: a prose register (British Carrier) uses zero staccato; a staccato register hammers. Long body sentences snap to a short punch. | 509/2559 sentences (20%) are 6 words or fewer. British Carrier: 0/27 entries with any short sentence; US Destroyer: every entry has at least 1. Act-break spike: "Same speed. Same turn. Same fight." | B long body, short hammers at the punch and act-break, match the register's staccato level. R "staccato sprinkled mid-body, or the punch is one long clause." G flag more than 3 consecutive short sentences outside a punch or act-break. | guidance |
| QL-14 | No intro or outro paragraph. Cold-open on entry 1 (hook, myth-correction, or date-context - never "welcome / today we'll cover"); fold the outro into the final entry's verdict as a bookend callback to entry 1. Any name/spec index is silent end-card data, not spoken. | Every analyzed script cold-opens on machine 1. Outro folded into the last entry: Kentucky BB bookends entry 1's Havana harbor; Vanguard names the 89-year span; B-21 bookends 1937/XB-15. | B no intro/outro; hook on entry 1; bookend in the last entry; emit any index as silent metadata. R "a welcome/today intro or standalone outro appeared - delete it, move the bookend into the final entry." G reject a spoken intro/outro; require the last entry to carry a bookend. | warn |
| QL-15 | Spec-block entries hit the beat spine: identity (name plus year plus role), spec block, production count, designed-vs-used twist, fate/legacy, verdict-punch. Order may flex; twist and punch are non-negotiable. Rebuild/argument entries may drop the spec block only. | Spine held across all every-built scripts; documented flex (B-17 twist-before-production, B-52 twist-after-fate). Spec block dropped only on rebuild entries (Tennessee BB). Twist and punch never dropped in 105 entries. | B hit the spine; reorder is allowed but never omit twist or punch; drop the spec only for rebuild/argument entries. R "missing a beat (no spec, no production, no fate) - add it or justify the drop." G reject entries missing twist or punch; warn a spec-block entry missing its spec or production count. | warn |

Backs the summary-ban (QL-5) mechanically: a closer that only re-uses body words fails novelty. | B write a verdict that turns, not one that recaps. | QL-18 | Every numeric claim is graded against the FULL locked-evidence set and must find two independent supporting sources; single-sourced numbers fail. G reject a numeric claim without two independent sources in the locked evidence. | hard-gate |
| QL-19 | Every claim is grounded against ALL locked-evidence segments; ungrounded claims fail (precise-or-silent, enforced in code). | In-flight. G reject an ungrounded claim. | hard-gate |

Lets a date-range or an appearance detail back a claim without being demanded as mandatory evidence. | B may cite timeframe/visual_identity as support. R do not flag a claim solely because its only support is timeframe/visual_identity. G accept these kinds as valid support in QL-18 and QL-19 grading. | guidance |
