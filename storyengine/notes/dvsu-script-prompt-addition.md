"EVERY [X] EVER BUILT" CENSUS FORMAT (applies when the title promises a complete inventory - Every/All [X] Ever Built):
- Entries run in strict chronological order. The FIRST entry doubles as the hook: its first sentence carries a superlative, origin claim, or contradiction ("The Boeing XB-15 first flew in 1937 as America's experimental leap into long-range strategic bombing."). No cold open, no preamble, ever.
- Each unit paragraph follows the six-beat anatomy, in roughly this order:
  1) IDENTITY - name + first flew/entered service + year + role or superlative claim.
  2) SPEC BLOCK - one sentence of exact numbers: "With a [N]-foot wingspan and [N] [engine] engines, it could carry [N] pounds of bombs at [N] mph over [N] miles." Adapt naturally for ships, tanks, and helicopters.
  3) PRODUCTION - "[Maker] built [N] between [YEAR] and [YEAR]." or "Only one prototype was built."
  4) DESIGNED-VS-USED TWIST - what it was built or believed to do, then what reality did to it. This is the channel's engine; never skip it.
  5) FATE/LEGACY - what replaced it, the lesson it left, or the record it set.
  6) VERDICT PUNCH - close with one to three staccato fragments or micro-sentences forming an ANTITHESIS or a triad: "The B-45 was first. The B-47 was better." / "Mach 2 wasn't cheap. Beauty doesn't pay the bills." / "Eight engines. Intercontinental range. The bomber that refuses to retire." Never a summary, never a restatement.
- NO REDUNDANCY - HARD RULE: no fact, claim, or clause may appear twice in one paragraph. The verdict punch must add a NEW turn of meaning, never repeat an earlier sentence in different words. Before finalizing, check the last sentence against every earlier sentence; if it restates one, rewrite it.
- Bridges: roughly one entry in three opens with a callback to the previous machine plus a pivot ("While Liberators filled the skies by the thousands, Douglas dreamed bigger."). The rest open clean per the opening-line rules.
- The final entry lands in the present day or bookends entry one ("Strategic bombing evolves, but never ends.").
- Rhythm: average sentence near thirteen words, with roughly a quarter of sentences at six words or fewer (the punch fragments). Long spec sentence, long spec sentence, short punch.
