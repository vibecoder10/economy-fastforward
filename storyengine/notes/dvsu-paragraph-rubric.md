# DvsU Paragraph Rubric - the Anton 6-beat test

Purpose: repeatable judge for whether a generated DvsU paragraph replicates Anton's
quality bar. Distilled 2026-07-15 from "Every US Strategic Bomber Ever Built.docx"
(23 entries, 2,313 VO words, avg 101 words/entry, range 81-111) plus battleship and
helicopter scripts for cross-checks. Source extracts: see TOP VIDEO SCRIPTS on the
Desktop; measured copies in the session scratchpad (bomber.txt).

## The 6 beats (every "Every X Ever Built" entry)

1. **Identity** - name + first flew/entered service + year + role or superlative claim.
   "The Boeing XB-15 first flew in 1937 as America's experimental leap into
   long-range strategic bombing."
2. **Spec block** - one sentence, exact numbers: "With a [N]-foot wingspan and [N]
   [engine] engines, it could carry [N] pounds of bombs at [N] mph over [N] miles."
3. **Production count** - "[Maker] built [N] between [YEAR] and [YEAR]." (or "Only
   one prototype was built.")
4. **Designed-vs-used twist** - what it was built to do, then what reality did to it.
   This is the channel's engine. (XB-15: built as a bomber, served as a transport.)
5. **Fate/legacy** - retirement, replacement, lesson, or record.
6. **Verdict punch** - 1-3 staccato fragments or micro-sentences closing the entry,
   an ANTITHESIS or triad, never a summary:
   - "The B-45 was first. The B-47 was better."
   - "Mach 2 wasn't cheap. Beauty doesn't pay the bills."
   - "Eight engines. Intercontinental range. The bomber that refuses to retire."
   - "The YB-60 was the safe choice. The B-52 was the right one."

## Hard pass/fail checks

- [ ] 95-120 spoken words (engine already enforces)
- [ ] All 6 beats present, in roughly this order
- [ ] Beat 6 is an antithesis/triad punch, NOT a restatement of an earlier sentence
- [ ] **No redundancy**: no fact or clause appears twice in the paragraph
      (failure example: current XB-19 output states "further development was
      uneconomical" twice)
- [ ] Real numbers in beats 2-3 (no "many", "several" where a count exists)
- [ ] Numbers as words for voiceover EXCEPT designations (B-52 stays B-52)
- [ ] No hype ("legendary", "iconic", "revolutionary", "game-changing")
- [ ] No Wikipedia opening ("The X was a [type] developed by [company] in [year]")
- [ ] Sentence rhythm: avg ~13 words, with short punch fragments (Anton: 28% of
      sentences are <=6 words); never 3+ long sentences in a row
- [ ] Name-opener budget respected (max 4-5 per 30 entries; slots enforced in code)
- [ ] Optional bridge (~1 in 3 entries): callback to previous machine + pivot
      ("While Liberators filled the skies by the thousands, Douglas dreamed bigger.")

## Anton's actual proof-machine paragraphs (positive controls)

**XB-15** (beats 1-2-3-4-5-6; twist = bomber-turned-transport):
> The Boeing XB-15 first flew in 1937 as America's experimental leap into long-range
> strategic bombing. With a 149-foot wingspan and four 850-horsepower Pratt & Whitney
> engines, this massive aircraft could carry 2,500 pounds of bombs over 5,130 miles.
> Only one prototype was built, but the XB-15 proved that large, multi-engine bombers
> could fly intercontinental distances. The aircraft served as a transport during
> World War II, hauling cargo across the Pacific. Though never used in combat as a
> bomber, the XB-15 validated concepts that would define American strategic aviation
> for the next eight decades.

**B-17** (twist = self-defense belief vs 47% loss rate; punch = "The price was
written in blood over German skies."):
> The Boeing B-17 Flying Fortress entered service in 1938 and became the backbone of
> America's daylight precision bombing campaign over Europe. At 74 feet long with four
> Wright Cyclone engines producing 1,200 horsepower each, the B-17 could carry 4,500
> pounds of bombs at 287 mph to targets 2,000 miles away. Bristling with thirteen
> .50-caliber machine guns, crews believed the aircraft could defend itself without
> fighter escort. Between 1938 and 1945, Boeing built 12,731 B-17s across all
> variants. The Eighth Air Force lost 4,735 B-17s over Europe, a staggering 47% loss
> rate among heavy bombers. The B-17 proved daylight precision bombing could work.
> The price was written in blood over German skies.

**B-52** (twist = designed to be obsolete by 1980, still flying; triad punch):
> The Boeing B-52 Stratofortress entered service in 1955 and became the longest-serving
> bomber in aviation history. With a 185-foot wingspan and eight Pratt & Whitney J57
> turbojets, the B-52 could carry 70,000 pounds of bombs at 650 mph over 8,800 miles.
> Boeing built 744 B-52s between 1952 and 1962. The B-52 dropped conventional bombs in
> Vietnam, launched cruise missiles in Iraq, and continues flying combat missions
> today. The aircraft was designed to be obsolete by 1980. Seventy years later, it
> still flies. The Air Force plans to operate B-52s until 2050, giving the
> Stratofortress a 95-year service life. Eight engines. Intercontinental range. The
> bomber that refuses to retire.

> further development of the plane uneconomical. The XB-19 spent months doing flight
> tests, and it had passageways inside the wings so crewmembers could service the
> engines in flight. The super-bomber was enormous, but further development was
> uneconomical.

Fails: beat order scrambled (fate before memorable fact), closer restates an earlier
clause verbatim (redundancy), no verdict punch (ends on a summary), no spec block,
no production count.

## Format variant note

"Most Hated X" entries swap beat 2 (spec block) for pilot-experience evidence
(after-action reports, groundings, lawsuits) and run ~130 words (105-146). Same
skeleton otherwise; beat 6 becomes concede-then-cut ("It was reliable enough to fly.
It was never reliable enough to relax.").
