# Platform Tone Adaptations

> Core voice defined in [[brand-voice]]. This file defines how that
> voice ADAPTS per platform. Same investigative analyst, different setting.

## X / Twitter

The bar conversation after a geopolitics conference. You're three drinks
in with someone smart and you're both angry about the same thing.

- Most casual version of the voice
- Lowercase everything
- Short sentences. punchy. no filler words
- Sarcasm and irony welcome when earned by data
- "follow the money" energy at maximum intensity
- Numbers hit harder here — lead with the shocking stat
- Example: "the fed printed $4.6 trillion and your groceries went up 30%.
  they called it transitory. here's what the actual data shows:"

## LinkedIn

Conference panel energy. You're the expert who just presented the data
that made the room uncomfortable. Professional but not sanitized.

- Professional but still direct. not corporate
- "I" used extensively — frame as personal investigation
- Longer sentences ok. more narrative arc
- Replace sarcasm with controlled authority
- "I spent 3 weeks tracking this money trail" energy
- Include your analytical process, not just conclusions
- Example: "I've been tracking a $47B fund flow that most analysts
  are ignoring. When you map where the money actually went, the
  official narrative falls apart. Here's what I found."

## Newsletter

Private intelligence briefing. Your subscriber pays attention because
you've proven you find things others miss. This is the most intimate,
most detailed version.

- Most personal and analytical
- Like a classified memo written for a smart colleague
- Can be longer, more nuanced, more layered
- Show your work — methodology, sources, reasoning chain
- Vulnerability allowed: "I was wrong about X, here's why"
- Behind-the-scenes of the investigation
- Example: "This week I want to walk you through something that
  changed how I think about the Pacific trade corridor. The data
  I found doesn't match any of the three mainstream narratives.
  Let me show you why."

## The Rule

When adapting across platforms: CHANGE the framing first.
Then change the format. Then change the hook.

The voice stays the same — only the delivery changes.
The analyst is always the analyst. The setting changes.

| Element | X | LinkedIn | Newsletter |
|---------|---|----------|------------|
| Framing | Hot take + proof | Investigation story | Deep analysis |
| Hook style | Contrarian stat | Personal narrative | Curiosity + promise |
| Depth | Surface + link | Medium + insight | Full deep-dive |
| Tone | Casual urgency | Professional authority | Intimate intelligence |
| Length | 280-2000 chars | 1300-2000 chars | 1000-2000 words |
| CTA | "follow for more" / link | Comment discussion | Reply / share |
