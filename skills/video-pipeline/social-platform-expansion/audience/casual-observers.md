# Audience: Casual Observers (Secondary)

> The "wait, that affects MY money?" audience segment.
> Curious about world events but not deep in geopolitics.
> They're discovering this world through our content.

## Who They Are

People who see a headline about trade wars or central bank
decisions and think "should I care about this?" They're not
policy wonks or finance professionals. They're smart people
in non-geopolitics fields who sense that global power shifts
affect their daily life but don't know how to connect the dots.

Demographics: 20-40, broader gender mix than enthusiasts,
primarily US/EU. Often in tech, creative, or service industries.
They follow a few geopolitics accounts but aren't deep in the space.

They found us through a viral tweet or a YouTube recommendation.
They stayed because we explained something complex in a way
that made them feel smarter, not dumber.

## What They Want

- **Personal stakes** — how does [geopolitical event] affect MY
  grocery bill, MY mortgage rate, MY job market? the bridge from
  "distant event" to "my wallet"
- **Simplified but not dumbed down** — explain the mechanism,
  don't just state the conclusion. they want to understand WHY,
  not just WHAT
- **"I can't believe I didn't know this"** moments — the feeling
  of seeing something clearly for the first time. the paradigm shift
- **Entry points** — where to start paying attention, what to
  watch, what to read next
- **Accessible numbers** — "$47B" is abstract. "$47B — enough to
  buy every NFL team twice" gives them scale

## What They Don't Want

- Jargon without explanation (basis points, yield curve inversion,
  repo market — explain or skip)
- Assumed knowledge ("as you know, the Bretton Woods system...")
- Fear mongering without actionable context
- Content that makes them feel stupid for not already knowing this
- Overly technical analysis with no personal relevance bridge

## How to Talk to Them

- Use the Personal Stakes Hook (ILLUSION_CONTROL) most often
- Bridge from their world to geopolitics: "your grocery bill
  went up 30% → here's the pipeline in the Strait of Hormuz
  that set the price"
- Analogies and scale comparisons: "$2.1T is more than the GDP
  of Canada"
- "Here's why you should care" framing (not condescending — 
  genuinely connecting dots they haven't seen)
- Encouraging, not intimidating: "you don't need a finance degree
  to follow the money. here's what to watch"

## Platform Behavior

- **X:** they like, retweet, but rarely reply with analysis.
  they share to their non-geopolitics followers. amplification engine
- **LinkedIn:** they share with "I never thought about it this way"
  framing. good for reach beyond our core niche
- **Newsletter:** they read but don't reply often. they're learning.
  the ones who stick become [[geopolitics-enthusiasts]] over time

## Conversion Path

Casual Observer → regular viewer → newsletter subscriber →
geopolitics enthusiast. This is a funnel. Social content aimed
at casuals is the TOP of the funnel. Don't optimize for their
engagement directly — optimize for converting them to enthusiasts.
